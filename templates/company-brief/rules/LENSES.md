# Lenses

`lens` picks the emphasis and the closing section (`angle`). When the value fits none of these cleanly, take the
closest, say which in `lens.interpretation`, and borrow from the others where it helps.

`angle.title` is the heading. Every point is `fact`, `inference` or `question`, cited when it is a fact.

## selling

Emphasis: buying signals (hiring in the relevant function, expansion, tooling changes, stated needs) and decision
makers. Closing section "Angle for selling": likely pains tied to the offer, who decides (from public roles only),
and three talking points. With no `your_company`, the pains are generic to the company's business and the section says so.

## customer_review

For an account the reader already has. Emphasis: results the company publishes, leadership changes, risk and expansion
signals. Closing section "Account review": expansion and churn-risk signals, a suggested agenda, and questions to ask.

## competing

Emphasis: positioning in the company's own words, review sentiment, price position. Closing section "Competitive
read": a comparison of positioning against `your_company` (where it is given; else against the category), and what it
means for the reader. Positioning claims come from the company's pages, cited as its claims.

## investing

Emphasis: market and why now, traction (customers, revenue or growth that is published), team backgrounds, competitors.
Closing section "Diligence questions": ten first-call questions, built from gaps and risks. No recommendation to invest
or not.

## swot

Emphasis: evidence for each quadrant. Closing section "SWOT": strengths, weaknesses, opportunities and threats as
cited points (an opinion with no source is an `inference` and says so), then the top three strategic questions.
Recommended moves are questions to test, not instructions.
