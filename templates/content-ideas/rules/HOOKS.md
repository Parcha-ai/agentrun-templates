# What makes a hook

The content-ideas skill reads this before every edition. Edit it like code. What the owner tells the agent later is
kept in its learning and wins over this file where they differ.

## A hook (one per idea)

- **news**: an event in the audience's industry, from the outlet that reported it.
- **report**: a new report, survey or dataset, read on the publisher's page.
- **regulation**: a rule, standard or policy change that touches the audience, from the regulator's or standard body's
  page, with its effective date.
- **platform_change**: a launch, pricing or policy change from a platform the audience uses, from its newsroom or
  changelog.
- **search_trend**: a rise in search interest you saw on a trends page. Trends values are a relative index (0 to 100)
  over a stated period and region, never a search volume.
- **event**: a dated conference, deadline or awareness day coming up.
- **discussion**: a community thread with real traction (subreddit or forum, points or comments, date).

## Not a hook

- An evergreen tip ("5 ways to close the books faster") with nothing dated behind it.
- A headline you saw only in a search result and did not open.
- Anything outside the window, unless it is still timely (an upcoming deadline, a discussion still growing) and dated.
- A tragedy, disaster, death or crime. Skip it, say so in `skipped`, and build nothing on it.
- The company's own past posts (they tell you what not to repeat).

## Labels

- `verified`: you read the primary page.
- `vendor_claim`: a vendor's own survey or claim about itself; say "vendor survey" in the text.
- `secondhand`: an outlet reporting someone else's figure you could not open; say whose figure it is.
- `rumor`: unconfirmed. Never build an idea that presents a rumor as fact.

## Credibility

- Every number keeps its source and period: "42% of controllers (Acme survey, fielded Sep 2026, n=1,200)".
- The company says only what it can back: no data, customers or results it has not published.
- Health, money, law and safety ideas point readers to the authoritative source and give no advice.
- Private people in threads are paraphrased, never named or linked.
