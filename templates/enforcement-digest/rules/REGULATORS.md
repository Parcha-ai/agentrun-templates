# Where regulators publish enforcement actions

Read the list page first, then the release or order behind each candidate. These are candidates, not a promise that
every page answers: a blocked page goes in `unreachable` with what it returned.

## How to read them

- A list page gives the date, the respondent and a one-line summary. The amount split, the findings status and the
  failing control live in the release or the order: open it before writing the action.
- An order's first pages carry the respondent, the findings (or "without admitting or denying"), and the sanctions
  section near the end carries the amounts and the remediation.
- A court-approved settlement has two dates: the agency's announcement and the court's approval. Keep both.

## Main enforcers by industry (when `regulators` is blank)

- Banks (US): OCC, Federal Reserve, FDIC, FinCEN, CFPB, OFAC, DOJ. Banks (UK): FCA, PRA.
- Broker-dealers and advisers (US): SEC, FINRA, CFTC for derivatives.
- Payments, money transmitters, crypto (US): FinCEN, CFPB, state regulators (NY DFS), OFAC, SEC, CFTC.
- Consumer lending (US): CFPB, FTC, state attorneys general.
- Data and privacy: FTC (US), ICO (UK), CNIL and the other EU data protection authorities.
