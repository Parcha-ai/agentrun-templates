## Bottom line
One randomized trial of SleepWell (n = 200) found a clear drop in insomnia severity against a waitlist, ISI mean
difference -5.3 (95% CI -7.2 to -3.4) [P2]. A 2025 meta-analysis of online CBT-I in general found a moderate to large
effect across nine trials but does not include SleepWell [P1]. Certainty for SleepWell itself is low.

## Key studies
| Paper | Design | n | Finding | Effect (95% CI) | Flags |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Okonkwo 2025 [P1] | meta-analysis, 9 trials | 2,840 | online CBT-I beats waitlist or usual care | SMD -0.76 (-0.95 to -0.57) | |
| Rask 2023 [P2] | RCT | 200 | SleepWell beats waitlist at 9 weeks | MD -5.3 (-7.2 to -3.4) | |
| Daniels 2024 [P3] | secondary analysis of P2 | same 200 | no age interaction (p = 0.41) | -6.0 and -4.8 by age | same trial as P2 |

## Evidence strength
| Outcome | Certainty | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| ISI at 9 weeks, SleepWell | low | one trial counted once, waitlist control, short follow-up |
| Insomnia severity, online CBT-I in general | moderate | meta-analysis, heterogeneity, indirect to SleepWell |

## Gaps
No active-control trial, no follow-up beyond nine weeks, no shift-worker data.

## Implications for the stated context
The evidence supports online CBT-I as a class and gives SleepWell one positive trial. It does not show SleepWell is
better than other programs or that the gain lasts.
