## Bottom line
The supplied fictional trial reports that SleepWell online CBT-I reduced insomnia severity compared with waitlist at nine weeks: mean difference **-5.3 Insomnia Severity Index (ISI) points**, 95% CI **-7.2 to -3.4**, with lower scores favoring SleepWell [P1]. This is evidence from **one randomized trial involving 200 adults**, reported in two papers—not two independent trials. Overall certainty is **low**, principally because the available captures are too brief to assess important safeguards against bias.

## Search strategy
The review followed three steps: (1) frame the program-specific question and look for existing syntheses; (2) search open indexes and registration records, check bibliographic and retraction information, and link duplicate trial reports; (3) extract the supplied results and assess certainty separately by outcome.

Searches ran on **2026-11-14**, with a default publication scope of 2011–2026. The question concerns adults receiving the specified fictional SleepWell program, compared with waitlist, with insomnia severity as the primary outcome. Other interventions called SleepWell were not treated as evidence about this program.

A Cochrane-targeted web search for `SleepWell CBT-I review` yielded no usable program-specific synthesis. Epistemonikos returned HTTP 403. Europe PMC's broad `SleepWell AND (insomnia OR CBT-I) AND (REVIEW)` query reported 89 hits, but this was a broad retrieval count rather than an eligible-study count; only the returned slice was inspected. Its exact `SW-2022-014` query found zero records. PubMed's `"SleepWell online CBT-I"` query also reported zero records, with a phrase-not-found warning. ClinicalTrials.gov returned no studies for the supplied registration identifier.

Crossref requests for both supplied DOIs returned HTTP 404. Exact-title retraction searches produced no matching notice, but these checks cannot establish the absence of corrections or retractions. Bibliographic details below therefore come from the supplied captured paper pages, not independent index confirmation. The two captures were used in place of fetching their URLs. Both are classified conservatively as **abstract-only**, meaning summary-level access rather than verified full text. No external paper or search snippet was used to supply an effectiveness finding.

## Synthesis
**Reported findings:** The primary estimate favors SleepWell and its confidence interval excludes no between-group difference [P1]. Both age-stratified estimates also favor the intervention, while the reported interaction test is not statistically significant, p=0.41 [P2]. No effects were pooled or recalculated for this review.

**Interpretation:** The evidence supports a short-term average advantage over waitlist in this sample. It does not show how SleepWell compares with another credible treatment. Nor does an average difference identify the probability that an individual participant will respond or achieve remission.

The younger subgroup's estimate is numerically more favorable, but the appropriate reported comparison of treatment effects across age groups is the interaction test—not whether either subgroup separately has a confidence interval excluding zero. The interaction result does **not** establish that younger adults benefit more. Conversely, failure to detect an interaction does **not** demonstrate equivalent effects across ages [P2].

There is no independent-study agreement or disagreement to assess. The overall and subgroup estimates are related results from one sample, and apparent consistency must not be presented as replication.

## Common measures
P1 identifies ISI as its primary outcome and states that lower scores are better [P1]. No clinically important difference threshold, responder definition, or remission criterion is supplied. Consequently, this review reports the published point difference without attaching an unsupported clinical-importance label or converting it into response rates.

## Gaps and future directions
The most immediate need is the complete trial report and a verifiable protocol or registration, allowing assessment of allocation procedures, prespecified outcomes, attrition, analysis population, and missing-data methods. Detailed eligibility criteria, baseline severity, recruitment setting, comorbidities, intervention content, and adherence would help assess applicability.

Independent randomized replication would address the lack of corroboration. Active-comparator trials, longer follow-up, and systematic harms reporting would answer questions the waitlist result cannot resolve. Age moderation requires a prespecified interaction analysis with an effect estimate and confidence interval, rather than conclusions based only on separate subgroup significance tests.

Search limitations include the blocked synthesis database, incomplete screening of broad Europe PMC results, unavailable Crossref records, and inability to inspect a matching registry entry. The bibliographic and registration limitations are explicit features of reviewing this fictional evidence set, not proof of misconduct or selective reporting.

## Implications for the stated context
The evidence supports a cautious statement that SleepWell reduced average insomnia severity relative to waitlist over nine weeks in the supplied trial. It does not establish durable benefit, comparative effectiveness against active treatment, safety, or an age-specific advantage. These conclusions summarize the supplied evidence and are not clinical advice.
